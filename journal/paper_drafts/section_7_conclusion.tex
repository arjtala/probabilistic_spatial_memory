\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\psm is a streaming spatial-temporal index for egocentric look-back QA: an H3
hex-grid tessellation with per-cell time-decayed HLL sketches, reservoir-sampled
exemplars, and a \caponek{} knob trading spatial diversity for
embedding-similarity precision. Its per-cell state is bounded and sublinear in
frame count, though total state grows with the number of visited cells and is not
globally memory-bounded (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
Our main result is a controlled, budget-matched characterization: under an
identical global exemplar budget, spatial allocation changes \emph{which} places
are remembered --- it protects rare/low-exposure places in a place-specific way
(beating coordinate-permutation nulls and robust to grid shifts) --- but does not
improve aggregate retrieval over budget-matched non-spatial baselines, and a
semantic-diversity coreset performs best overall (\S\ref{sec:results-main}). We
present the spatial policy as an experimental probe, not a deployed method. We
also correct the record on prior framing: query-aware retrieval beats uniform
frame sampling but does not show MLLMs require \psm{}
(\S\ref{sec:results-vanilla-mllm}); the S22 deployment figure and the HDD
corpus-scale cardinality/AUC claims are withdrawn (\S\ref{sec:results-hdd},
\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Follow-up directions.}
A truly globally-budgeted allocator integrated into the C engine and evaluated
end-to-end; human-authored, video-verified look-back QA; and engine-backed
multi-session HDD evaluation once drive-level features are extracted. The engine
(${\sim}700$ LoC of C) and the budget-matched evaluation harness are released
under MIT.\footnote{Anonymized for double-blind review:
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/probabilistic_spatial_memory-3C17/}
(\texttt{reproduce\_paper.sh} regenerates the headline figures and
Table~\ref{tab:multi-corpus-h3}).}
