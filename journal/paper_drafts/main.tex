\begin{abstract}
An agent on wearable glasses must remember its environment under a fixed
on-device memory budget, yet standard retrieval banks grow linearly with
recording time. We study a concrete question that this raises but that prior
work leaves untested: \emph{under a fixed global exemplar budget, when does
spatial allocation preserve useful experiences better than non-spatial
streaming memory?} We introduce \textbf{Probabilistic Spatial Memory} (\psm), a
streaming spatial-temporal index over an H3 hex grid with a per-cell
reservoir of embedding exemplars and a time-decayed HyperLogLog ring. Its
``bounded memory'' refers to the per-cell summary: revisits do not accumulate
every frame, but expansion into new cells grows total state, which is \emph{not}
bounded independently of recording duration (\S\ref{sec:method}).

Separately from the C engine, our main contribution is a controlled,
\textbf{budget-matched} study in a Python harness on 14 street-scale egocentric
sessions (3 corpora, 2 capture/localization stack families
~\cite{dai2023sloper4d,pan2025lookout}, CLIP-L), comparing retention strategies
at an identical global exemplar budget and candidate count: global
reservoir, FIFO, uniform-time sampling, a semantic $k$-center coreset, and a
causal spatial-allocation probe. The finding is a characterization, not a win:
\textbf{spatial allocation changes \emph{which} places are remembered rather
than improving aggregate retrieval.} Correct spatial alignment benefits
rare/low-exposure-place recall relative to coordinate-permutation nulls
(significant on 2 of 3 null seeds; robust to grid translation/rotation), but
this trades against common-place recall, and a non-spatial semantic-diversity
coreset performs best overall (\S\ref{sec:results-main}). The spatial policy is
a Python experimental probe with area-growing bookkeeping, not part of the C
engine or a deployed method. We
also correct the record on prior framing: a frontier MLLM comparison measures
query-aware retrieval vs.\ uniform sampling (not that MLLMs require \psm), and
on multi-hour driving (Honda HDD~\cite{ramanishka2018hdd}) we report the
corrected per-cell memory model as modeled logical state, deferring
engine-backed multi-session cardinality/retrieval to future work
(\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}). The C engine ($\sim$700 LoC) and the budget-matched
harness (policies, semantic/coordinate-null controls, paired statistics) are
released under MIT.

\end{abstract}
