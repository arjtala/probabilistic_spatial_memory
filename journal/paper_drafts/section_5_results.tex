\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate fixed-budget retention, the released \psm{} engine, and auxiliary
retrieval baselines for egocentric look-back QA.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:results-setup}

The primary study uses 14 street-scale sessions from three corpora and two
capture/localization stack families: Nymeria, SLOPER4D, and
LookOut~\cite{ma2024nymeria,dai2023sloper4d,pan2025lookout}. We use
CLIP-L~\cite{clip-l-laion,radford2021clip} (768-d, 1\,fps), $K{=}5$, and H3
$r{=}12$. Hit@5 counts a hit when any returned timestamp lies in the ground-truth
interval; mIoU@5 scores $\pm1.5$\,s exemplar windows. Nymeria intervals accompany
third-person action narrations, while SLOPER4D and LookOut questions were
generated from target frames. Both are retrieval proxies rather than
human-authored look-back QA (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Fixed-budget allocation: which places get remembered}
\label{sec:results-main}

Our primary experiment isolates \emph{retention allocation} from encoder quality
and exemplar payload. Every policy receives an identical global exemplar budget
$M{=}128$ and returns the same number of candidates ($K{=}5$, global cosine, no
per-cell cap), so retrieval differences reflect only \emph{which} frames a policy
retains. The match covers embedding-plus-timestamp bytes; spatial counters remain
area-growing bookkeeping, disclosed rather than counted as a global system budget.
On the 14 sessions (5 seeds, CLIP-L, $r{=}12$), we compare a streaming
global reservoir (Algorithm~R), FIFO, a half-reservoir/half-recent hybrid,
offline uniform-time sampling, and an offline
\texttt{semantic\_kcenter} coreset (farthest-first in cosine space; a
diversity diagnostic, not a streaming competitor), and \texttt{spatial\_priority},
a causal exact-$M$ policy that protects under-represented cells. Sessions are the
unit of analysis (seeds averaged within session, then paired session bootstrap).
Alongside Hit@5/mIoU@5 we report \emph{oracle retention coverage} (any retained
timestamp in a GT interval, before ranking) and pre-registered rare/common-place
strata.

\paragraph{Policy and stratum protocol.}
Frames arrive in timestamp order. The spatial-priority probe maintains per-cell
banks summing exactly to $M$: a frame from an under-represented cell evicts a
uniformly chosen exemplar from a most-represented cell; once balanced, Algorithm~R
replacement runs within that cell. If occupied cells outnumber slots, seed-stable
random cell priorities avoid a recency cache. The policy cannot recover discarded
frames and is therefore causal but not equivalent to an offline stratified sample.
For each question, its target place is the modal canonical H3 cell in the
ground-truth interval; rare/common are the bottom/top quartiles of that cell's frame
exposure within the session. These labels remain fixed in controls. Coordinate
nulls permute frame-to-coordinate alignment (three seeds); boundary controls
translate the grid 4.5\,m east/north or rotate its local tangent plane $30^\circ$.

The result is a characterization, not a win (Table~\ref{tab:fixed-budget},
Fig.~\ref{fig:fixed-budget}).
\textbf{Spatial allocation changes which places are remembered, not aggregate
retrieval.} Paired against the budget-matched \texttt{global\_reservoir},
\texttt{spatial\_priority} is statistically flat on Hit@5 ($-0.3$\,pp, 95\% CI
$[-2.3,2.0]$) and mIoU ($-0.0009$ $[-0.015,0.014]$), but shifts the composition of
what is recalled: rare/low-exposure places $+4.4$\,pp $[-1.3,10.3]$ at the cost of
common places $-2.7$\,pp $[-5.1,-0.4]$. The rare-place effect is \emph{place-specific}:
correct spatial alignment beats coordinate-permutation nulls on rare-place recall
(perm202 $+8.3$\,pp $[4.2,12.7]$, perm303 $+7.7$\,pp $[1.5,13.9]$ significant;
perm101 $+4.0$\,pp $[-0.3,8.6]$ marginal) and is robust to grid translation and
$30^\circ$ rotation (Hit@5 $\Delta{\approx}0$, all n.s.), so it is not a
partition-balancing or H3-boundary artifact. Crucially, a non-spatial coreset
(\texttt{semantic\_kcenter}) is \emph{best overall} and beats
\texttt{spatial\_priority} on aggregate Hit@5 ($+4.1$\,pp $[1.5,6.7]$): generic
visual diversity, not physical space, explains the strongest budget-matched
retention. At a tighter budget ($M{=}64$) the spatial policy's rare-place and
aggregate edge over the global reservoir grows (a secondary budget interaction),
but it still trails \texttt{uniform\_time}/\texttt{semantic\_kcenter} overall
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fixed-budget}A). We therefore present \texttt{spatial\_priority} as
an experimental retention probe and report all baselines and controls in the main
comparison.

\subsection{Engine and retrieval checks}
\label{sec:results-engine}

The fixed-budget policies above run in the released Python evaluation harness;
\texttt{spatial\_priority} is not integrated into the C engine. We separately
check the engine on Nymeria. On \texttt{shelby\_arroyo\_act0} (187 intervals),
the capped engine ($R{=}128$, \caponek{}$=K$) obtains 11.2\% Hit@5 versus 13.4\%
for the full CLIP-L bank; with eviction disabled ($R{=}1024$), it recovers the
same 25/187 hits and mIoU, an implementation check. Across all 30 Nymeria
sessions and five seeds, Hit@5 increases monotonically with \caponek{} at both
reservoir sizes and the eviction-free endpoint again matches brute force;
reducing $R$ from 1024 to 128 costs 1.37\,pp at \caponek{}$=K$. A 10\,s
sliding-window CLIP baseline is 1.0\,pp better than the capped endpoint but keeps
the full frame bank. Finally, an optional MLLM reranker cannot change any @$k$
set metric because it only permutes the top five. Its rank-sensitive Hit@1 effect
is mixed across sessions, so we do not use it by default. These checks diagnose
the substrate; they are not evidence that the spatial allocation probe wins.

\subsection{Memory, latency, and failure modes}
\label{sec:results-memory-latency}

Each saturated CLIP-L cell costs about 0.38\,MiB for $R{=}128$ exemplars plus
0.06\,MiB for the $C{=}60$, $p{=}10$ HLL ring. Unlike a dense $N$-frame bank,
this is independent of dwell time but not of the number of occupied cells; on
short Nymeria sessions the dense bank is still cheaper, and on wide-area HDD the
ring can dominate (\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}). At the target CLIP-L
configuration ($d{=}768$, $R{=}128$, $C{=}60$), a host benchmark with 20k
observations over 500 cells uses ${\sim}96$\,MiB RSS and answers an uncentered
global-scan query in ${\sim}13$\,ms; centered k-ring queries scan fewer cells. A
previously reported Galaxy S22 result used a much smaller configuration
($d{=}128$, $R{=}4$, $C{=}12$), so we withdraw it as a deployment measurement
and defer a true-configuration phone rerun (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Cross-corpus resolution sensitivity}
\label{sec:results-multi-corpus}

As an auxiliary check, we sweep H3 resolution across the same 14 sessions and
three encoder families: CLIP-L, CLIP-bigG, and
SigLIP-2-large~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2}. A session passes when Hit@5 is non-decreasing over
$r\in\{10,11,12\}$ with at least a 4\,pp $r10{\to}r12$ lift. Pre-registered
CLIP-L passes on 9/14 sessions (Table~\ref{tab:multi-corpus-h3}); the post-hoc
any-encoder disjunction reaches 13/14 and is only an optimistic upper bound.
\texttt{BurlingameDT4\_feb5} fails for all encoders. Because SLOPER4D/LookOut
questions are target-frame-generated, this tests resolution sensitivity, not
held-out QA or the fixed-budget allocation claim.

\subsection{Query-aware retrieval vs.\ uniform frame sampling}
\label{sec:results-vanilla-mllm}

On the same 14 sessions, Gemini 3.1 Pro chooses a ground-truth frame from eight
uniform samples only ${\sim}5\%$ of the time. Any-of-eight coverage, independent
of Gemini's choice, is ${\sim}8\%$ and is an upper bound on what this candidate
set permits. Query-guided \psm{} top-5 retrieval exceeds that coverage on nearly
every session, despite returning fewer frames. This supports only the narrow
claim that query-aware retrieval beats uniform sampling; it does not show that
MLLMs require \psm{}.

\subsection{Multi-session revisit structure and memory accounting (Honda HDD)}
\label{sec:results-hdd}

Honda HDD~\cite{ramanishka2018hdd} provides 132 drives (102.9\,h with valid RTK
GPS) over eight months. Because it is driving rather than wearable capture and
has no look-back QA labels, we use only its trajectory to characterize revisit
structure and analytical memory, not retrieval accuracy. At H3 $r{=}10$
(${\sim}66$\,m), 47.0\% of (drive, cell) traversal events occur in cells visited
on at least two distinct days: 5{,}163 revisited cells with a median revisit span
of 106 days (maximum 221). Figure~\ref{fig:hdd-combined} models logical state from
the RTK trajectory. Although the $R{=}128$ exemplar term is capped per cell,
allocating a $C{=}60$, 60\,KiB HLL ring to each of 23{,}298 visited cells adds
${\sim}1.4$\,GB, making full \psm{} larger than the dense bank at 1\,fps (2.27
versus 1.14\,GB). Only 20\% of cells are seen by the temporal midpoint, so state
continues to grow with explored area; these are modeled bytes, not measured RSS
or a global memory bound. Corpus-scale CLIP features are unavailable beyond one
sanity drive, and the earlier cardinality/AUC results used offline substitutes
rather than the released engine; we therefore withdraw them and make no
engine-backed HDD retrieval or cardinality claim (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
