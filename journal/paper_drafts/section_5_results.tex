\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate \psm{} against a brute-force CLIP oracle and a frontier MLLM on egocentric look-back QA (retrieval baselines---sliding-window and uniform-sample CLIP---are in \S\ref{sec:supp-ablations}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:results-setup}

We evaluate on the Nymeria atomic-action benchmark~\cite{ma2024nymeria}, 30 sessions (${\sim}5{,}600$ narrations) on Project Aria Gen~1 headsets~\cite{engel2024aria}; each narration's $[t_\text{start},t_\text{end}]$ interval is retrieval ground truth---a proxy benchmark, as the narrations are third-person action descriptions, not natural look-back questions (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).
Primary results use \texttt{shelby\_arroyo\_act0} (187 narrations, 69\,m bbox), encoder CLIP-L~\cite{clip-l-laion,radford2021clip} (768-d, 1\,fps).
\psm{} uses the \S\ref{sec:method} defaults ($r{=}12$, $R{=}128$); an oracle row at $R{=}1024$ isolates reservoir eviction from the \caponek{} effect, and the baseline is brute-force CLIP top-5 over the full $N$-frame bank (no spatial structure, no capping).
We report three top-5 metrics: Hit@5 (any exemplar timestamp in a GT interval), exemplar mIoU@5 (IoU of exemplar $\pm1.5$\,s windows vs.\ GT), and bucket mIoU@5 (IoU of each cell's $[t_\text{min},t_\text{max}]$ envelope vs.\ GT).

\subsection{Single-session operating points}
\label{sec:results-main}

On the \texttt{shelby\_arroyo\_act0} proxy session (187 narrations), at the deployment point ($R{=}128$, \caponek{}$=K$), \psm{} reaches $83\%$ of brute-force Hit@5 ($11.2\%$ vs $13.4\%$; mIoU $0.063$ vs $0.074$) at $8\times$ smaller per-cell state; at $R{=}1024$ it recovers the \emph{same 25/187} with identical mIoU---a substrate-correctness check whose $2.2$\,pp/$0.011$ gap to $R{=}128$ is the bounded-memory cost (full operating-point sweep in supp.\ Table~\ref{tab:headline}).
An optional MLLM rerank adds localization precision, not recall: \caponek{}$=1$+rerank reaches exemplar mIoU $0.069$, statistically indistinguishable from brute-force's $0.074$ (paired bootstrap $\Delta{=}{-}0.005$, 95\% CI $[-0.032,+0.021]$; \S\ref{sec:results-mllm-rerank}).
The strict-diversity cost, the brute-force ceiling, and the operating-point Pareto, retrieval baselines, memory/H3-resolution ablation, and 30-session monotonicity sweep are in the supplement (\S\ref{sec:supp-ablations}).

\psm{}'s per-cell state is session-length-invariant (${\sim}0.44$\,MiB at $R{=}128$) while brute-force grows linearly in $N$; on the short Nymeria sessions brute-force is still marginally cheaper (the synthetic crossover falls at $N\approx1500$ frames, supp.\ Fig.~\ref{fig:f6-memory}), so the bounded-memory payoff is demonstrated on the multi-hour HDD corpus (\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}). Retrieval is sub-millisecond either way (host-CPU state size, latency, and the shared-miss failure analysis in supp.\ \S\ref{sec:supp-memory-latency}).
Representative retrievals---\psm{}'s top-5 candidates with the Gemini-selected frame, spanning two hits (one a Gemini Hit@1 rescue) and an honest revisit-heavy miss---are in supp.\ Fig.~\ref{fig:f7-qualitative}.

\subsection{Generalization, and why MLLMs need a spatial prefilter}
\label{sec:results-vanilla-mllm}

The spatial-axis result generalizes beyond the headline session: across
\textbf{14 street-scale sessions spanning 3 sensor stacks} (SLOPER4D~\cite{dai2023sloper4d},
Nymeria, LookOut~\cite{pan2025lookout}) \textbf{and 3 encoder families}
(CLIP-L, CLIP-bigG, SigLIP-2~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2}), \textbf{13/14 pass a
pre-registered monotone-$r10{\to}r12$ Hit@5 criterion} (SigLIP-2 strongest at
11/14; the lone failure is an honest one,
\S\ref{sec:limitations})---full criterion and per-session verdicts in supp.\
\S\ref{sec:supp-multicorpus} (Table~\ref{tab:multi-corpus-h3}).
\label{sec:results-multi-corpus}
On these same 14 sessions we ask whether a frontier MLLM can retrieve
\emph{without} a spatial prefilter: we run Gemini 3.1 Pro at $K{=}8$
uniformly-sampled frames per question (supp.\ Fig.~\ref{fig:f2-psm-vs-mllm}), matching
\psm{}'s candidate budget. \psm{} matches or exceeds its Hit@5 on every session,
and \textbf{two sessions land at vanilla MLLM $=0\%$} because the $K{=}8$ uniform
frames never fall inside the ${\sim}3$\,s GT intervals. The failure is one of
temporal coverage, not reasoning---a larger context window cannot recover signal
uniform sampling never captured (mechanism in supp.\ \S\ref{sec:results-mllm-rerank})---the
spatial-temporal blind spot \psm{}'s cell-targeted prefilter is designed to
close.

\subsection{Multi-session persistence at metropolitan scale (Honda HDD)}
\label{sec:results-hdd}

The results so far are single-pass, so they cannot exercise the multi-session
persistence and revisit-cardinality properties (\S\ref{sec:intro}) that motivate
a bounded index. We close this gap on the Honda Research Institute Driving
Dataset (HDD)~\cite{ramanishka2018hdd}: 104\,h of San Francisco Bay Area driving
with RTK GPS and a front camera (our extraction covers 132 drives, 102.9\,h with
valid GPS), which over 8 months genuinely re-drives the same streets. HDD ships
no look-back QA labels, so these results are \emph{systems} and
\emph{self-supervised}, not retrieval-accuracy (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Revisit structure and area-bounded state (Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}A).}
Binning every drive to H3 $r{=}10$ (${\sim}66$\,m), \textbf{47.0\%} of
(drive, cell) traversal events fall in cells revisited on $\geq2$ distinct days
(pre-registered bar $\geq30\%$), across 5{,}163 revisited cells spanning a
\textbf{median of 106 days} (max 221; arterial, not a depot artifact---46.5\%
excluding the 20 most-driven cells). Over these 102.9\,h and 23{,}298 distinct
cells, \psm{}'s per-cell-capped state ($\sum_\text{cells}\min(\text{frames}, R)$)
is smaller than a dense per-frame bank at every ingest rate, the gap widening as
the reservoir discards redundant dwell/revisit frames
($1.3$/$2.3$/$4.3$/$7.2\times$ at 1/5/15/30\,fps). State thus grows
\emph{sublinearly in observation count}---$O(\text{distinct cells})$, not
$O(\text{frames})$, and not a plateau (only 20\% of cells seen by the halfway
mark): the empirical form of ``budgeted by area, not sequence length'' (cf.\ the
synthetic crossover, supp.\ Fig.~\ref{fig:f6-memory}).

\paragraph{Persistent, retrievable cross-session memory (Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}B,C).}
Feeding a revisited cell's frames chronologically across drives, a single fixed
$1\,\mathrm{KiB}$ ($p{=}10$) sketch accrues to \textbf{8{,}112 unique
observations over 219 days} while its 60-slot ring-buffer windowed estimate
decays between visits and jumps on each revisit---the ``decay while maintaining
unique-event cardinality across revisits'' behavior of \S\ref{sec:intro} (a
Nymeria micro-eval, \S\ref{sec:results-hll}, validates per-session accuracy; a
Python reimplementation matches the C sketch to $2.4\times10^{-5}$ median
relative error). Is that memory retrievable across sessions? Label-free,
querying a revisited cell with a drive-A exemplar, different-day drive-B frames
outrank other-cell frames at cross-session AUC \textbf{0.853} (5{,}222 cells,
2{,}000 stratified queries) vs.\ a shuffled-cell null of 0.498 and a same-drive
upper bound of 0.977---strong cross-session place recognition from CLIP cosine
alone. The same-cell-vs-all split blends geographic proximity with visual
identity ($k$-nearest-cell control deferred, \S\ref{sec:limitations}); AUC (not
top-$k$ hit-rate) is the discriminability measure here, as the 357k-frame pool
is near-duplicate-dominated.
