\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate \psm{} against brute-force CLIP-cosine retrieval and a frontier MLLM on egocentric look-back QA (retrieval baselines---sliding-window and uniform-sample CLIP---are in \S\ref{sec:results-ablations}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:results-setup}

We evaluate on the Nymeria atomic-action benchmark~\cite{ma2024nymeria}, 30 sessions (${\sim}5{,}600$ narrations) on Project Aria Gen~1 headsets~\cite{engel2024aria}; each narration's $[t_\text{start},t_\text{end}]$ interval is retrieval ground truth---a proxy benchmark, as the narrations are third-person action descriptions, not natural look-back questions (no purpose-built outdoor egocentric look-back QA benchmark exists; \S\ref{sec:conclusion}).
Primary results use \texttt{shelby\_arroyo\_act0} (187 narrations, 69\,m bbox), encoder CLIP-L~\cite{clip-l-laion,radford2021clip} (768-d, 1\,fps).
\psm{} uses the \S\ref{sec:method} defaults ($r{=}12$, $R{=}128$); a full-reservoir row at $R{=}1024$ isolates reservoir eviction from the \caponek{} effect, and the baseline is brute-force CLIP-cosine top-5 over the full $N$-frame bank (no spatial structure, no capping).
We report three top-5 metrics: Hit@5 (any exemplar timestamp in a GT interval), exemplar mIoU@5 (IoU of exemplar $\pm1.5$\,s windows vs.\ GT), and bucket mIoU@5 (IoU of each cell's $[t_\text{min},t_\text{max}]$ envelope vs.\ GT).

At the deployment point ($R{=}128$, \caponek{}$=K$), \psm{} reaches $84\%$ of brute-force Hit@5 ($11.2\%$ vs $13.4\%$; mIoU $0.063$ vs $0.074$) with session-length-invariant per-cell state; at $R{=}1024$ ($8\times$ the per-cell reservoir) it recovers the \emph{same 25/187} with identical mIoU---a substrate-correctness check whose $2.2$\,pp/$0.011$ gap to $R{=}128$ is the bounded-memory cost. On this single 187-question session the $2.1$\,pp Hit@5 gap sits within one binomial standard error ($95\%$ Wilson CIs $[7.5,16.6]$ and $[9.2,19.0]$ overlap heavily), so we treat the seed-averaged 30-session sweep (\S\ref{sec:results-ablations}) as the aggregate claim and this table as the mechanism-isolating anatomy.
Strict spatial diversity (\caponek{}$=1$) costs $5.4$\,pp at $R{=}1024$ ($8.0\%$ vs $13.4\%$; $4.3$\,pp at $R{=}128$) because revisits to a cell collapse into one bucket---the intended behavior for place-distinct queries.\footnote{Table~\ref{tab:headline} is a single representative seed; its one non-monotonicity---\caponek{}$=1$ Hit@5 higher at $R{=}128$ ($9.1\%$) than $R{=}1024$ ($8.0\%$)---is within reservoir-sampling noise (max-cosine need not be the ground-truth frame, so a random subsample can incidentally surface a GT-interval exemplar the full set's top-1 misses); the seed-averaged 30-session sweep (\S\ref{sec:results-ablations}) is monotone in $R$ at every \caponek{}.}
Brute-force itself caps at $13.4\%$: 162/187 narrations are revisit-heavy and visually indistinguishable to CLIP cosine~\cite{weller2025limit}.
An optional MLLM rerank adds localization precision, not recall---\caponek{}$=1$+rerank reaches exemplar mIoU $0.069$, statistically indistinguishable from brute-force's $0.074$ (\S\ref{sec:results-ablations}).
The operating-point Pareto, non-spatial retrieval baselines, memory/H3-resolution ablation, and the seed-averaged 30-session sweep are detailed next (\S\ref{sec:results-ablations}).

\subsection{Ablations, retrieval baselines, and reranking}
\label{sec:results-ablations}

\paragraph{Operating-point Pareto and non-spatial baselines.}
As \caponek{} sweeps from 1 to $K$, Hit@5 rises and bucket mIoU@5 falls monotonically---the place-diversity/embedding-precision trade the knob is designed to expose. Against non-spatial baselines on the headline session, sliding-window CLIP at $\ell{=}10$\,s edges \psm{} \caponek{}$=K$ by $+1.6$\,pp Hit@5 ($15.0\%$ vs $13.4\%$) by averaging out per-frame variance, but requires the full $N$-frame bank; uniform-sample CLIP forms the floor at $4.3$--$8.6\%$. Tightening the reservoir from $R{=}1024$ to $128$ costs $2.2$\,pp Hit@5 at \caponek{}$=K$, while H3 resolution barely moves Hit@5 (${\pm}0.5$\,pp across $r\in\{10,11,12,13\}$) but moves bucket mIoU monotonically ($0.004\to0.019$).

\paragraph{30-session monotonicity.}
The single-seed headline (Table~\ref{tab:headline}) generalizes to a seed-averaged sweep over all 30 Nymeria sessions. Mean Hit@5 climbs monotonically in \caponek{} at both reservoirs---$2.99\%\to4.81\%\to6.11\%\to7.55\%$ at $R{=}128$ and $3.26\%\to5.69\%\to7.08\%\to8.92\%$ at $R{=}1024$---with the $R{=}1024$ endpoint matching brute-force exactly and the sweep monotone in $R$ at every \caponek{} (e.g.\ $3.26\%$ vs $2.99\%$ at \caponek{}$=1$). This confirms the lone single-seed non-monotonicity in Table~\ref{tab:headline} is reservoir noise. Reservoir eviction thus costs $1.37$\,pp Hit@5 in the 30-session aggregate at \caponek{}$=K$; sliding-window CLIP exceeds both by $+1.0$\,pp.

\paragraph{Optional MLLM rerank.}
An MLLM reranker permutes \psm{}'s top-5, so Hit@5 is invariant by construction; it adds localization precision, not recall. On the headline session it lifts exemplar mIoU@5 from $0.074$ to $0.101$ ($+36.5\%$ relative) at \caponek{}$=K$, and \caponek{}$=1$+rerank reaches mIoU $0.069$, statistically indistinguishable from brute-force's $0.074$ (paired bootstrap $\Delta{=}{-}0.005$, 95\% CI $[-0.032,+0.021]$, $n{=}187$). On Hit@1 the effect is bimodal---it tripled Hit@1 on \texttt{seq008\_running\_001} ($10\%{\to}30\%$) and helped on \texttt{seq009} ($+3.6$\,pp), but hurt on \texttt{seq003} ($-3.3$\,pp) and Nymeria ($-1.6$\,pp). The rerank is a visually-rich-query lever, counterproductive on action-narrations; we do not promote it to a default, and \psm{}'s headline result holds without any MLLM in the loop.

\subsection{Memory, latency, and failure modes}
\label{sec:results-memory-latency}

\psm{}'s per-cell state is session-length-invariant ($R{=}128\times768$-d$\times4$\,B $\approx0.38$\,MiB plus a 60-slot $p{=}10$ HLL ring $\approx0.06$\,MiB), whereas brute-force grows linearly in $N$.
On the short Nymeria sessions ($N\in[748,1325]$, ${\sim}22$\,min) brute-force is still marginally cheaper---the synthetic crossover falls just beyond this range at $N\approx1500$ frames (${\sim}25$\,min), past which the gap reaches ${\sim}10$--$20\times$ at multi-hour streams---so the bounded-memory payoff is demonstrated on the multi-hour HDD corpus (\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}), where \psm{} is smaller at every ingest rate.
Retrieval is sub-millisecond either way: brute-force dot-product over the 1133-frame bank is 67\,$\mu$s median, and the paired in-process \psm{} benchmark is 456/153\,$\mu$s at \caponek{}$=1$/$K$ (host-CPU, not wearable-device; \S\ref{sec:limitations}).
Counterintuitively, \caponek{}$=1$ is the slower setting: its top-$K$ results are $K$ \emph{distinct} cells, so each triggers a separate time-envelope merge-window pass (the query's dominant cost), whereas \caponek{}$=K$'s results often share a cell and reuse a single merge---cosine scoring itself is identical across settings.
On a Galaxy S22 (Exynos~2200, aarch64, Cortex-X2 prime core), the same synthetic engine benchmark ingests at $0.86\,\mu$s/frame and answers a location query in $104\,\mu$s and a semantic query in $1.16/1.75$\,ms (\caponek{}$=1$/$K$, 1024-tile state) with $20.2$\,MiB peak RSS---a $2.6$--$3.5\times$ slowdown vs.\ the \emph{same} synthetic bench on host ($0.33\,\mu$s/frame, $30\,\mu$s, $0.37/0.55$\,ms; distinct from the Nymeria in-process figures above), confirming the microsecond host-CPU constants carry to wearable-class ARM at sub-2\,ms, tens-of-MiB cost (engine core only; CLIP encoding and video I/O excluded, as on host, \S\ref{sec:limitations}). On this sparse 1024-tile workload \caponek{}$=1$ is \emph{faster} than \caponek{}$=K$---opposite the dense in-process Nymeria bench above---because \caponek{}$=K$'s larger candidate sort, not the per-cell merge-window, dominates.
Of the 162 shared brute-force/\psm{} misses, most are revisit-heavy narrations with recurring near-identical frames; the 25 groundable questions reference temporally-distinct events, so the shared $13.4\%$ ceiling reflects single-frame CLIP-cosine matching---sliding-window averaging and SigLIP-2 exceed it (\S\ref{sec:results-ablations}, \S\ref{sec:results-multi-corpus})---not method quality (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Cross-corpus, cross-encoder generalization}
\label{sec:results-multi-corpus}

The spatial-axis result generalizes beyond the headline session. To rule out a
single-corpus / single-encoder artifact, we replicate it across \textbf{14
street-scale sessions spanning 3 sensor stacks} (SLOPER4D~\cite{dai2023sloper4d}:
head-mounted LiDAR + DJI Action2; Nymeria: Project Aria + MPS SLAM;
LookOut~\cite{pan2025lookout}: Project Aria across 5 Bay Area locations)
\textbf{and 3 encoder families} (CLIP-L 768-d, CLIP-bigG 1280-d, SigLIP-2-large
1024-d~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2}). For each (session, encoder) the acceptance
criterion is Hit@5 non-decreasing across $r\in\{10,11,12\}$ with $\geq4$\,pp lift
from $r{=}10$ to $r{=}12$ (conservative against binomial noise at 28--30
questions/session); a session passes if any encoder satisfies both.
\textbf{13/14 sessions pass} (Table~\ref{tab:multi-corpus-h3}): cross-corpus
robustness holds (SLOPER4D 3/3, Nymeria 1/1, LookOut 9/10) despite different
sensor stacks and question pipelines; SigLIP-2 is the strongest single encoder
(11/14 vs 9/14 CLIP-L, 10/14 bigG), with two rescues where its sigmoid-pairwise
recipe recovers spatial signal the CLIP family misses on visually-repetitive
trajectories; the lone failure, \texttt{BurlingameDT4\_feb5}, fails all three
encoders (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). No existing egocentric benchmark combines
outdoor spatial trajectories with temporally-grounded text, so we assemble a
three-corpus scaffold with per-corpus extractors while stopping short of claiming
a released benchmark.

\subsection{Why MLLMs need a spatial prefilter}
\label{sec:results-vanilla-mllm}

On these same 14 sessions we ask whether a frontier MLLM can retrieve
\emph{without} a spatial prefilter. We run Gemini 3.1 Pro at $K{=}8$
uniformly-sampled frames per question (Fig.~\ref{fig:f2-psm-vs-mllm}), matching
\psm{}'s candidate budget to isolate the retrieval substrate. \psm{} matches or
exceeds the MLLM's \emph{any-of-$K$ coverage}---a hit if any of the $K{=}8$
uniform frames falls in a GT interval, an upper bound on Gemini's single-frame
pick---on every session (median ${\sim}4\times$) and never loses (the sole tie is
Hillsdale6 at $13.8\%$, also \psm{}'s weakest cross-encoder session);
\textbf{two sessions sit at $0\%$} because the $K{=}8$ uniform frames never
include a frame inside the ${\sim}3$\,s GT intervals.
The failure is architectural, not a matter of scale. At 100--640\,s session
durations the uniform-frame gap is 12--80\,s---a ${\sim}4\%$ chance of
\emph{seeing} the right frame before any discrimination applies. Gemini's actual
single-frame pick rate is just $7.1\%$, far below even a generous any-of-$K$
coverage upper bound of $21.4\%$ at $K{=}32$ (the re-scored Hit@5 our baseline
reports): a $3\times$ discrimination gap compounding the coverage
gap. A larger context window thus cannot recover signal that uniform sampling
never captured---the spatial-temporal blind spot \psm{}'s cell-targeted prefilter
is designed to close.

\subsection{Multi-session persistence at metropolitan scale (Honda HDD)}
\label{sec:results-hdd}

The results so far are single-pass, so they cannot exercise the multi-session
persistence and revisit-cardinality properties (\S\ref{sec:intro}) that motivate
a bounded index. We close this gap on the Honda Research Institute Driving
Dataset (HDD)~\cite{ramanishka2018hdd}: 104\,h of San Francisco Bay Area driving
with RTK GPS and a front camera (our extraction covers 132 drives, 102.9\,h with
valid GPS), which over 8 months genuinely re-drives the same streets. HDD ships
no look-back QA labels, so these results are \emph{systems} and
\emph{self-supervised}, not retrieval-accuracy (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
Driving is not wearable capture, but it supplies what no egocentric look-back
corpus yet offers---months of GPS-anchored revisits to the same places---and the
properties we test (area-bounded state, cross-session cardinality accrual) turn
on revisit \emph{structure}, not locomotion mode; pedestrian and indoor wearers
revisit space at finer spatial scale, if anything tightening the
$O(\text{area})$ argument (cf.\ the GPS-anchored wearable walk in
Fig.~\ref{fig:f8-psm-viz}).

\paragraph{Revisit structure and area-bounded state (Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}A).}
Binning every drive to H3 $r{=}10$ (${\sim}66$\,m), \textbf{47.0\%} of
(drive, cell) traversal events fall in cells revisited on $\geq2$ distinct days
(pre-registered bar $\geq30\%$), across 5{,}163 revisited cells spanning a
\textbf{median of 106 days} (max 221; arterial, not a depot artifact---46.5\%
excluding the 20 most-driven cells). Over these 102.9\,h and 23{,}298 distinct
cells, \psm{}'s per-cell-capped state ($\sum_\text{cells}\min(\text{frames}, R)$)
is smaller than a dense per-frame bank at every ingest rate, the gap widening as
the reservoir discards redundant dwell/revisit frames
($1.3$/$2.3$/$4.3$/$7.2\times$ at 1/5/15/30\,fps). State thus grows
\emph{sublinearly in observation count}---$O(\text{distinct cells})$, not
$O(\text{frames})$, and not a plateau (only 20\% of cells seen by the halfway
mark): the empirical form of ``budgeted by area, not sequence length'' (cf.\ the
synthetic crossover at $N\approx1500$ frames, \S\ref{sec:results-memory-latency}).

\paragraph{Persistent, retrievable cross-session memory (Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}B,C).}
Feeding a revisited cell's frames chronologically across drives, a single fixed
$1\,\mathrm{KiB}$ ($p{=}10$) sketch accrues to \textbf{8{,}112 unique
observations over 219 days} while its 60-slot ring-buffer windowed estimate
decays between visits and jumps on each revisit---the ``decay while maintaining
unique-event cardinality across revisits'' behavior of \S\ref{sec:intro} (a
Nymeria micro-eval validates per-session cardinality accuracy---median $1.0\%$
relative error across cells, comfortably below HLL's theoretical relative
standard error of $1.04/\sqrt{2^{10}}\approx3.25\%$ at $p{=}10$ (a standard
error, not a hard bound), accurate enough to support counting queries such as ``how many distinct people
did I interact with in the kitchen?'' natively on the substrate---without a
downstream MLLM recounting frames; and a Python
reimplementation matches the C sketch to $2.4\times10^{-5}$ median
relative error, confirming this is the engine's behavior, not an analysis
artifact). Is that memory retrievable across sessions? Label-free,
querying a revisited cell with a drive-A exemplar, different-day drive-B frames
outrank other-cell frames at cross-session AUC \textbf{0.853} (5{,}222 cells,
2{,}000 stratified queries) vs.\ a shuffled-cell null of 0.498 and a same-drive
upper bound of 0.977---a label-free cross-session retrieval signal from CLIP
cosine alone. We report this as a byproduct evidencing that the substrate
preserves cross-session identity, not as a visual place recognition (VPR)
result: the same-cell-vs-all split blends geographic proximity with visual
identity, so purpose-built VPR
descriptors~\cite{keetha2023anyloc,alibey2023mixvpr} and a $k$-nearest-cell
control (\S\ref{sec:related}, \S\ref{sec:limitations}) are the proper baselines,
deferred to future work.\footnote{AUC
(not top-$k$ hit-rate) is the discriminability measure here, as the 357k-frame
pool is near-duplicate-dominated.}
