\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate \psm{} against a brute-force CLIP oracle and a frontier MLLM on egocentric look-back QA (retrieval baselines---sliding-window and uniform-sample CLIP---are in \S\ref{sec:supp-ablations}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:results-setup}

We evaluate on the Nymeria atomic-action benchmark~\cite{ma2024nymeria}, 30 sessions (${\sim}5{,}600$ narrations) collected with Project Aria Gen~1 headsets~\cite{engel2024aria}; each narration's $[t_\text{start}, t_\text{end}]$ interval serves as retrieval ground truth---a proxy benchmark, since the narrations are third-person action descriptions, not natural look-back questions (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).
Primary results use \texttt{shelby\_arroyo\_act0} (187 narrations, 69\,m bbox).
The encoder is CLIP-L~\cite{clip-l-laion,radford2021clip}, 768-d, 1\,fps, 11.6\,ms per query on CPU.
\psm{} runs at H3 resolution~12 (${\sim}9.4$\,m hexes), time window 30\,s, ring-buffer capacity 60, HLL precision $p{=}10$, reservoir $R{=}128$ exemplars per cell; we also report an oracle row at $R{=}1024$ to isolate reservoir eviction from the \caponek{} effect.
The baseline is brute-force CLIP top-5 over the full $N$-frame embedding bank (no spatial structure, no capping).
We report three top-5 metrics: Hit@5 (did any exemplar timestamp fall inside a GT interval), exemplar mIoU@5 (mean IoU between exemplar $\pm1.5$\,s windows and GT), and bucket mIoU@5 (mean IoU between each cell's $[t_\text{min}, t_\text{max}]$ envelope and GT).
Hit@5 captures retrieval recall; exemplar mIoU, localization precision; bucket mIoU, temporal-envelope overlap.

At the deployment point ($R{=}128$, \caponek{}$=K$), \psm{} reaches $83\%$ of brute-force Hit@5 ($11.2\%$ vs $13.4\%$; mIoU $0.063$ vs $0.074$) at $8\times$ smaller per-cell state; at $R{=}1024$ it recovers the \emph{same 25/187} with identical mIoU---a substrate-correctness check whose $2.2$\,pp/$0.011$ gap to $R{=}128$ is the bounded-memory cost.
Strict spatial diversity (\caponek{}$=1$) costs $5.4$\,pp at $R{=}1024$ ($8.0\%$ vs brute-force $13.4\%$; $4.3$\,pp at $R{=}128$) because revisits to a cell collapse into one bucket---the intended behavior for place-distinct queries.
Brute-force itself caps at $13.4\%$: 162/187 narrations are revisit-heavy and visually indistinguishable to CLIP cosine~\cite{weller2025limit}.
MLLM rerank adds localization precision, not recall (exemplar mIoU $0.074\to0.101$, $+37\%$; Hit@5 unchanged at 25/187); \caponek{}$=1$+rerank reaches mIoU $0.069$, statistically indistinguishable from brute-force ($0.074$; paired bootstrap $\Delta{=}{-}0.005$, 95\% CI $[-0.032,+0.021]$).
Additional ablations (operating-point Pareto, retrieval baselines, memory/H3-resolution ablation, and the 30-session monotonicity sweep) are in the supplement (\S\ref{sec:supp-ablations}).

\subsection{Memory, latency, and failure modes}
\label{sec:results-memory-latency}

\psm{}'s per-cell state is session-length-invariant ($R{=}128\times768$-d$\times4$\,B $\approx0.38$\,MiB plus a 60-slot $p{=}10$ HLL ring $\approx0.06$\,MiB), whereas brute-force grows linearly in $N$.
On the short Nymeria sessions ($N\in[748,1325]$, ${\sim}22$\,min) brute-force is still marginally cheaper---the synthetic crossover falls just beyond this range at $N\approx1500$ frames (supp.\ Fig.~\ref{fig:f6-memory})---so the bounded-memory payoff is demonstrated on the multi-hour HDD corpus (\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}), where \psm{} is smaller at every ingest rate.
Retrieval is sub-millisecond either way: brute-force dot-product over the 1133-frame bank is 67\,$\mu$s median, and the paired in-process \psm{} benchmark is 456/153\,$\mu$s at \caponek{}$=1$/$K$ (host-CPU, not wearable-device; \S\ref{sec:limitations}).
Of the 162 shared brute-force/\psm{} misses, most are revisit-heavy narrations with recurring near-identical frames; the 25 groundable questions reference temporally-distinct events, so the shared $13.4\%$ ceiling is benchmark hardness, not method quality (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Multi-corpus, multi-encoder generalization}
\label{sec:results-multi-corpus}

To rule out a single-corpus / single-encoder artifact, we replicate the
spatial-axis result across \textbf{14 street-scale sessions spanning 3 sensor
stacks} (SLOPER4D~\cite{dai2023sloper4d} head-mounted LiDAR, Nymeria Project
Aria + MPS, LookOut~\cite{pan2025lookout} Project Aria across 5 Bay Area sites)
\textbf{and 3 encoder families} (CLIP-L, CLIP-bigG, SigLIP-2-large~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2}).
Under a pre-registered criterion---Hit@5 non-decreasing across $r\in\{10,11,12\}$
with $\geq4$\,pp lift from $r{=}10$ to $r{=}12$ on at least one encoder
(conservative against binomial noise at 28--30 questions/session)---\textbf{13/14
sessions pass} (SLOPER4D 3/3, Nymeria 1/1, LookOut 9/10), with SigLIP-2 the
strongest single encoder (11/14), rescuing two visually-repetitive sessions
where neither CLIP-family encoder passes. The sole failure,
\texttt{BurlingameDT4\_feb5}, is a visually-homogeneous trajectory where
captioned queries do not discriminate at cell scale (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
Per-(session, encoder) verdicts and the assembly of this three-corpus scaffold
(no released benchmark yet combines outdoor trajectories with temporally-grounded
text) are in the supplement (Table~\ref{tab:multi-corpus-h3}).

\subsection{Vanilla MLLM as retrieval: why MLLMs need a prefilter}
\label{sec:results-vanilla-mllm}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/f2_psm_vs_mllm.pdf}
\caption{Vanilla Gemini 3.1 Pro at $K{=}8$ uniform frames (red) vs \psm{} \texttt{clipL@r12} top-5 (blue) on every v1 session.
\psm{}'s query-guided retrieval reaches every session the uniform baseline misses; four sessions land at vanilla MLLM $= 0\%$ because the $K{=}8$ uniform frames never include a GT-interval frame.}
\label{fig:f2-psm-vs-mllm}
\end{figure}

We run Gemini 3.1 Pro on all 14 sessions at $K{=}8$ uniformly-sampled frames per question (Fig.~\ref{fig:f2-psm-vs-mllm}), matching \psm{}'s candidate budget to isolate the retrieval substrate.
\psm{} matches or exceeds the MLLM's Hit@5 on every session (median ${\sim}4\times$) and never loses (the sole tie is Hillsdale6 at $13.8\%$, also \psm{}'s weakest cross-encoder session); \textbf{four sessions land at vanilla MLLM $=0\%$} because the $K{=}8$ uniform frames never include a frame inside the ${\sim}3$\,s GT intervals.
The root cause is temporal coverage: at 100--640\,s session durations the uniform-frame gap is 12--80\,s, a ${\sim}4\%$ chance of \emph{seeing} the right frame before any discrimination applies.
The failure is architectural, not a matter of scale: at $K{=}32$ the oracle upper bound is $21.4\%$ but Gemini's actual pick rate stays $7.1\%$---a $3\times$ discrimination gap compounding the coverage gap, so a larger context window cannot recover signal that uniform sampling never captured.
This is the spatial-temporal blind spot that \psm{}'s cell-targeted prefilter is designed to close.

\subsection{Multi-session persistence at metropolitan scale (Honda HDD)}
\label{sec:results-hdd}

The results so far are single-pass---each session is traversed once---so they
cannot exercise the multi-session persistence and revisit-cardinality
properties (\S\ref{sec:intro}) that motivate a bounded spatial-temporal index.
We close this gap on the Honda Research Institute Driving Dataset
(HDD)~\cite{ramanishka2018hdd}, 104\,h of San Francisco Bay Area driving with
real RTK GPS and a front camera; our extraction covers 132 drives (102.9\,h
with valid GPS). A vehicle sweeps far more area per minute than a walker, and
over 132 drives spanning 8 months genuinely re-drives the same streets. HDD
ships no look-back QA labels, so we report \emph{systems} and
\emph{self-supervised} results here, not a retrieval-accuracy metric
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Revisit structure.}
Binning every drive to H3 $r{=}10$ (${\sim}66$\,m), \textbf{47.0\%} of
(drive, cell) traversal events fall in cells revisited on $\geq2$ distinct days
(against a pre-registered $\geq30\%$ acceptance bar), across 5{,}163 revisited
cells whose first-to-last visit spans a \textbf{median of 106 days} (max 221).
The coverage is arterial, not a depot artifact: excluding the 20 most-driven
cells leaves it at 46.5\%. This is the genuine multi-session substrate the
single-pass corpus lacks.

\paragraph{State scales with area, not time (Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}A).}
Over 102.9\,h and 23{,}298 distinct $r{=}10$ cells, we compare \psm{}'s
per-cell-capped state ($\sum_\text{cells} \min(\text{frames in cell}, R)$
exemplars) against a dense bank that stores every frame.
\psm{} is smaller at every ingest rate, and the gap widens with frame rate as
the reservoir discards more redundant dwell/revisit frames:
$1.3\times$/$2.3\times$/$4.3\times$/$7.2\times$ smaller at 1/5/15/30\,fps,
capping 26/57/77/86\,\% of frames. The mechanism is that state grows
\emph{sublinearly in observation count}---it is $O(\text{distinct cells})$, not
$O(\text{frames})$. We stress this is \emph{not} an observed memory plateau: on
this exploratory fleet corpus cumulative area keeps growing (only 20\% of cells
are seen by the halfway mark), so the saving comes from per-cell capping and is
therefore ingest-rate dependent. This is the empirical form of the abstract's
``budgeted by area, not sequence length,'' complementing the synthetic crossover
of supp.\ Fig.~\ref{fig:f6-memory}.

\paragraph{Cross-session cardinality accrual and decay (Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}B).}
A Nymeria micro-eval (\S\ref{sec:results-hll}, supp.) validates the HLL sketch's per-session accuracy; HDD's
revisits let us exercise it \emph{across sessions}. Feeding a revisited cell's
frames in chronological order across drives, a single fixed $1\,\mathrm{KiB}$
($p{=}10$) sketch accrues to \textbf{8{,}112 unique observations over 219 days}
(15 of 16 revisit days), while the windowed estimate over its 60-slot ring
buffer (\S\ref{sec:results-memory-latency}) decays between visits and jumps on
each revisit---the ``decay while maintaining unique-event cardinality across
revisits'' behavior of \S\ref{sec:intro}. A Python reimplementation matches the
C-engine sketch to $2.4\times10^{-5}$ median relative error on this real data,
confirming the accrual is the engine's behavior, not an analysis artifact.

\paragraph{The persistent memory is retrievable across sessions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}C).}
Finally, is a place recorded on one day retrievable from another? Without any
labels, we query a revisited cell with an exemplar from drive~A and measure the
AUC that different-day drive-B frames in that cell outrank frames from other
cells. Over 5{,}222 revisited cells (2{,}000 stratified queries) the
cross-session AUC is \textbf{0.853}, against a shuffled-cell null of 0.498 and a
same-drive upper bound of 0.977---strong cross-session place recognition purely
from CLIP cosine over the persistent per-cell exemplars.
The positive/negative split (same cell vs.\ all other cells) blends coarse
geographic proximity with visual identity; a $k$-nearest-cell hard-negative
control would isolate the latter (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). Literal top-$k$
hit-rate is low ($\leq0.07$@10) because the 357k-frame pool is dominated by
near-duplicate same-drive frames, so AUC, not top-$k$, is the discriminability
measure here.
