\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Benchmark ceiling and coverage.}
Both \psm{} and brute-force CLIP plateau at 13.4\% Hit@5 on the headline session
(\S\ref{sec:results-main}): 162/187 narrations describe repeatedly-entered rooms
with near-identical frames, so single-frame CLIP-cosine retrieval converges to a
frame rarely matching the annotator's single chosen interval. This is a ceiling
of single-frame cosine matching, not an absolute benchmark limit (sliding-window
CLIP ($15.0\%$) and SigLIP-2 rescues clear it; \S\ref{sec:results-ablations},
\S\ref{sec:results-multi-corpus}), and \psm{} and brute-force agree on the 25
uniquely-groundable questions. Future benchmarks should
score against \emph{any} acceptable revisit window. Relatedly, the deepest
single-session analysis (Table~\ref{tab:headline}) remains one Nymeria session,
and the 14-session sweep (\S\ref{sec:results-multi-corpus}) uses a heuristic
monotonicity-plus-lift criterion rather than a formal test; mIoU/counting across
all 14 are a follow-up.

\paragraph{One honest spatial-axis failure.}
Of the 14 street-scale sessions, \texttt{BurlingameDT4\_feb5} (242\,m bbox) is
the only one where all three encoders fail the monotone-\caponek{} lift: a
visually homogeneous parking-lot street whose queries collapse onto a few
indistinguishable cells. The same failure recurs on Aria Gen~2
Pilot's~\cite{aria-gen2-pilot} GPS-grounded walks, confirming the bottleneck is
encoder discrimination, not trajectory quality.

\paragraph{Evaluation scope.}
\psm's index latency is measured on host \emph{and} on a Galaxy S22 (Exynos~2200)
engine-core benchmark (\S\ref{sec:results-memory-latency}); the remaining
on-device gap is the CLIP encoder (the heavy component) still unmeasured on
wearable silicon. An efficient distilled encoder (MobileCLIP~\cite{vasu2024mobileclip}
or an AM-RADIO/E-RADIO agglomerative model~\cite{ranzinger2024radio}) is the
natural drop-in to close that gap, since \psm{} is encoder-agnostic
(\S\ref{sec:results-multi-corpus}). Our baselines omit a random-cell / nearest-GPS control, so spatial indexing is
not fully isolated from within-cell embedding similarity, though query-guided
retrieval beating uniform sampling (\S\ref{sec:results-vanilla-mllm}) and bucket
mIoU's H3-granularity sensitivity (\S\ref{sec:results-ablations}), neither from
similarity alone, partially separate the axes; the control is future work.

\paragraph{HDD is a systems, not a retrieval-accuracy, result.}
HDD ships no look-back QA labels, so the multi-session results
(\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}) are \emph{systems} and \emph{self-supervised}
(bounded state, cross-session cardinality, label-free place retrieval), not a
Hit@5/mIoU number: a deliberate division of labor in which the wearable corpora
carry retrieval accuracy and HDD carries multi-session persistence. Two caveats:
the cross-session split (same $r{=}10$ cell vs.\ all others) blends geographic
proximity with visual identity, so a $k$-nearest-cell hard-negative control and
a comparison against purpose-built VPR
descriptors~\cite{keetha2023anyloc,alibey2023mixvpr} (\S\ref{sec:related}) are
the natural next steps; and the extracted features carry a ${\sim}3$\,s
GPS-warmup skew (${\sim}17$\,m median) that we correct at analysis time
(shifting AUC by only $+0.005$, $0.848{\to}0.853$), with the source-side fix
verified in simulation but not yet re-run through GPU extraction.
