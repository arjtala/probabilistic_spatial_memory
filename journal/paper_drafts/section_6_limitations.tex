\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Benchmark ceiling on revisit-heavy narrations.}
Both \psm and brute-force CLIP plateau at 13.4\% Hit@5 on the headline session
(\S\ref{sec:results-main}): 162/187 narrations describe rooms entered
repeatedly, producing near-identical frames on which both converge to a frame
rarely matching the annotator's single chosen interval. This is benchmark
hardness, not method quality---the two agree on the 25 uniquely-groundable
questions and differ only on the ambiguous remainder. Future benchmarks should
score against \emph{any} acceptable revisit window.

\paragraph{One honest spatial-axis failure.}
Of the 14 street-scale sessions (\S\ref{sec:results-multi-corpus}),
\texttt{BurlingameDT4\_feb5} (242\,m bbox) is the only one where all three
encoders fail the monotone-\caponek lift---a visually homogeneous parking-lot
street whose queries collapse onto a few indistinguishable cells. The same
failure recurs on Aria Gen~2 Pilot's~\cite{aria-gen2-pilot} GPS-grounded walks,
confirming the bottleneck is encoder discrimination, not trajectory quality.

\paragraph{Latency scope.}
\psm's latency (\S\ref{sec:results-memory-latency}) combines a 1024-tile
dim-128 synthetic CPU stress test (${\sim}700\,\mu$s) and the real Nymeria state
(7 cells, dim\,768); both are host-CPU sanity checks, not on-device claims. The
wearable deployment target is unmeasured, though porting the C engine is
straightforward.

\paragraph{No spatial-only baseline.}
Our baselines (brute-force, sliding-window, uniform-sample CLIP) omit a
spatial-only heuristic (nearest-GPS, random-cell). The gains thus show
query-guided cell retrieval beats uniform temporal coverage at matched budget,
but do not fully isolate spatial indexing from embedding similarity within the
selected cells.

\paragraph{Multi-corpus coverage.}
The deepest single-session analysis (Table~\ref{tab:headline}) remains Nymeria
\texttt{shelby\_arroyo\_act0}; the 14-session sweep
(\S\ref{sec:results-multi-corpus}) reports Hit@5 under a heuristic
monotonicity-plus-lift criterion rather than a formal test, and mIoU/counting
across all 14 are a follow-up.

\paragraph{HDD is a systems, not a retrieval-accuracy, result.}
HDD ships no look-back QA labels, so the multi-session results
(\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}) are \emph{systems} and \emph{self-supervised}
(bounded state, cross-session cardinality, label-free place recognition), not a
Hit@5/mIoU number---a deliberate division of labor in which the wearable corpora
carry retrieval accuracy and HDD carries multi-session persistence.
Two caveats. First, F-HDD-3's positive/negative split (same $r{=}10$ cell vs.\
all other cells) blends coarse geographic proximity with visual place identity;
a $k$-nearest-cell hard-negative control would isolate the visual component and
is the natural next step. Second, the extracted HDD feature files carry a
${\sim}3$\,s GPS-warmup clock skew (${\sim}17$\,m median); we correct it at
analysis time by re-aligning per-frame coordinates from the raw RTK track, and
verified the correction shifts cross-session AUC by only $+0.005$
($0.848{\to}0.853$). The source-side extraction fix is verified in simulation
but has not been re-run through GPU extraction.
