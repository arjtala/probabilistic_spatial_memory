\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Benchmark ceiling and coverage.}
Both \psm{} and brute-force CLIP plateau at 13.4\% Hit@5 on the headline session
(\S\ref{sec:results-main}): 162/187 narrations describe repeatedly-entered rooms
with near-identical frames, so both rarely match the annotator's single chosen
interval---benchmark hardness, not method quality (future benchmarks should
score against \emph{any} acceptable revisit window). The 14-session sweep uses a
heuristic monotonicity-plus-lift criterion, not a formal test, and one session
(\texttt{BurlingameDT4\_feb5}, a visually homogeneous parking-lot street) is an
honest cross-encoder failure---also recurring on Aria Gen~2
Pilot's~\cite{aria-gen2-pilot} walks, so the bottleneck is encoder
discrimination, not trajectory quality.

\paragraph{Evaluation scope.}
\psm's latency (supp.\ \S\ref{sec:supp-memory-latency}) is a host-CPU sanity
check, not an on-device claim, and our baselines omit a spatial-only heuristic,
so the gains show query-guided retrieval beats uniform temporal coverage at
matched budget but do not fully isolate spatial indexing from embedding
similarity. HDD ships no look-back QA labels, so its results
(\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}) are \emph{systems} and \emph{self-supervised}, not
Hit@5/mIoU; the cross-session split blends geographic proximity with visual
identity (a $k$-nearest-cell control is the next step), and the features carry a
${\sim}3$\,s GPS-warmup skew corrected at analysis time (AUC $0.848{\to}0.853$),
with the source-side fix verified in simulation but not yet re-extracted.
