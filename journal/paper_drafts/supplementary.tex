Beyond the single-session headline (\S\ref{sec:results-main}), four patterns:
(1)~\emph{Operating-point Pareto}: as \caponek{} sweeps from 1 to $K$, Hit@5 rises and bucket mIoU falls monotonically. Strict spatial diversity (\caponek{}$=1$) costs $5.4$\,pp at $R{=}1024$ ($8.0\%$ vs brute-force $13.4\%$; $4.3$\,pp at $R{=}128$) because revisits to a cell collapse into one bucket---the intended behavior for place-distinct queries. Brute-force itself caps at $13.4\%$: 162/187 narrations are revisit-heavy and visually indistinguishable to CLIP cosine~\cite{weller2025limit}.
(2)~\emph{Retrieval baselines}: sliding-window CLIP @ $\ell{=}10$\,s exceeds \psm{} \caponek{}$=K$ by $+1.6$\,pp Hit@5 ($15.0\%$ vs $13.4\%$) by averaging out per-frame variance, but requires the full $N$-frame bank; uniform-sample forms the floor at $4.3$--$8.6\%$.
(3)~\emph{Memory ablation}: tightening the reservoir from 1024 to 128 exemplars costs $2.2$\,pp Hit@5 at \caponek{}$=K$; H3 resolution barely moves Hit@5 (${\pm}0.5$\,pp across $r \in \{10,11,12,13\}$) but moves bucket mIoU monotonically ($0.004 \to 0.019$).
(4)~\emph{30-session monotonicity}: in the matched $R{=}128$ deployment sweep, mean Hit@5 climbs monotonically from $2.99\%$ at \caponek{}$=1$ through $4.81\%$ and $6.11\%$ to $7.55\%$ at \caponek{}$=K$. The $R{=}1024$ full-reservoir sweep follows the same shape ($3.26\% \to 5.69\% \to 7.08\% \to 8.92\%$) and matches brute-force exactly at the eviction-free endpoint; sliding-window CLIP exceeds both by $+1.0$\,pp. Thus, at \caponek{}$=K$, reservoir eviction costs $1.37$\,pp in the 30-session aggregate.

\subsection{Synthetic memory-scaling crossover}
\label{sec:supp-memory}
Fig.~\ref{fig:f6-memory} plots per-method embedding memory against session length
on the 30 Nymeria sessions. \psm{}'s per-cell-capped state is constant in $N$
while brute-force grows linearly; on these short sessions ($N{\le}1325$,
${\sim}22$\,min) brute-force is still marginally cheaper, with the crossover at
$N\approx1500$ frames (${\sim}25$\,min) under the observed ${\sim}10$-cell
footprint, past which the gap reaches ${\sim}10$--$20\times$ at multi-hour
streams. The real-data form of this bound---where \psm{} is smaller at every
ingest rate---is demonstrated on the 102.9\,h HDD corpus
(\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}, Fig.~\ref{fig:hdd-combined}A).

\subsection{Memory, latency, and failure modes (single-session)}
\label{sec:supp-memory-latency}
\psm{}'s per-cell state is session-length-invariant ($R{=}128\times768$-d$\times4$\,B $\approx0.38$\,MiB plus a 60-slot $p{=}10$ HLL ring $\approx0.06$\,MiB), whereas brute-force grows linearly in $N$.
On the short Nymeria sessions ($N\in[748,1325]$, ${\sim}22$\,min) brute-force is still marginally cheaper---the synthetic crossover falls just beyond this range at $N\approx1500$ frames (Fig.~\ref{fig:f6-memory})---so the bounded-memory payoff is demonstrated on the multi-hour HDD corpus (\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}), where \psm{} is smaller at every ingest rate.
Retrieval is sub-millisecond either way: brute-force dot-product over the 1133-frame bank is 67\,$\mu$s median, and the paired in-process \psm{} benchmark is 456/153\,$\mu$s at \caponek{}$=1$/$K$ (host-CPU, not wearable-device; \S\ref{sec:limitations}).
Of the 162 shared brute-force/\psm{} misses, most are revisit-heavy narrations with recurring near-identical frames; the 25 groundable questions reference temporally-distinct events, so the shared $13.4\%$ ceiling is benchmark hardness, not method quality (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Full multi-corpus, multi-encoder per-session verdicts}
\label{sec:supp-multicorpus}
This is the full form of the generalization result summarized in
\S\ref{sec:results-multi-corpus}. To rule out a single-corpus / single-encoder
artifact, we replicate the spatial-axis result across 14 street-scale sessions
spanning 3 sensor stacks (SLOPER4D~\cite{dai2023sloper4d}: head-mounted LiDAR +
DJI Action2; Nymeria: Project Aria + MPS SLAM; LookOut~\cite{pan2025lookout}:
Project Aria across 5 Bay Area locations) and 3 encoder families (CLIP-L 768-d,
CLIP-bigG 1280-d, SigLIP-2-large 1024-d~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2}). To our
knowledge no existing egocentric benchmark combines outdoor spatial trajectories
with temporally-grounded text annotations; we therefore assemble a practical
three-corpus scaffold with per-corpus extractors for LiDAR-SLAM, Aria MPS, and
Aria VRS trajectories, while stopping short of claiming a released benchmark.
Acceptance criterion: for each (session, encoder), Hit@5 must be non-decreasing
across $r\in\{10,11,12\}$ with $\geq4$\,pp lift from $r{=}10$ to $r{=}12$; a
session passes if any encoder satisfies both (the $4$\,pp threshold is
conservative against binomial noise at 28--30 questions per session).
Table~\ref{tab:multi-corpus-h3} gives the per-(session, encoder) verdicts.
Three patterns: (a)~cross-corpus robustness holds (SLOPER4D 3/3, Nymeria 1/1,
LookOut 9/10) despite different sensor stacks and question pipelines;
(b)~SigLIP-2 is the strongest single encoder (11/14 vs 9/14 clipL, 10/14 bigG),
with two rescues (\texttt{Sanmateopark\_garage}, \texttt{Hillsdale6}) where the
sigmoid-pairwise recipe picks up spatial signal the CLIP family misses on
visually-repetitive trajectories; (c)~the lone failure,
\texttt{BurlingameDT4\_feb5}, fails all three encoders
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{HLL cardinality estimation (Nymeria micro-eval)}
\label{sec:results-hll}

To validate that \psm{}'s 60\,KiB per-cell state provides accurate cardinality tracking alongside mergeable time windows, we conducted a micro-evaluation on the Nymeria \texttt{shelby\_arroyo\_act0} session. For each cell, we extract the true count of distinct frames ingested and compare it against the HLL estimate from the C engine's ring buffer ($p{=}10$, or $1\,\mathrm{KiB}$ sketches). We report at H3 resolution~10 to ensure cells accumulate enough frames (270--593 here) for a meaningful cardinality check; at the paper's default $r{=}12$, individual cells see fewer frames but the per-sketch error bound holds by construction. Table~\ref{tab:hll-micro} shows that the relative error tightly bounds to the expected theoretical limit (${\sim}3.2\%$), demonstrating that the data structure accurately supports counting queries (``how many distinct people did I interact with in the kitchen?'') natively on the substrate without requiring a downstream MLLM to recount frames. For the HDD cross-session accrual (\S\ref{sec:results-hdd}), a Python reimplementation of the sketch matches the C engine to $2.4\times10^{-5}$ median relative error, confirming the accrual is the engine's behavior, not an analysis artifact.

\subsection{PSM $\to$ MLLM rerank}
\label{sec:results-mllm-rerank}

\paragraph{Why vanilla MLLM sampling fails (\S\ref{sec:results-vanilla-mllm}).}
The failure of $K$-uniform Gemini is architectural, not a matter of scale. At
100--640\,s session durations the uniform-frame gap is 12--80\,s, a ${\sim}4\%$
chance of \emph{seeing} the right frame inside a ${\sim}3$\,s GT interval before
any discrimination applies. At $K{=}32$ the oracle upper bound is $21.4\%$ (the
any-of-$K$ Hit@5 our re-scored baseline summaries report) but Gemini's actual
pick rate---counting only the single frame it selects---stays $7.1\%$, a
$3\times$ discrimination gap compounding the coverage gap, so a larger context
window cannot recover signal that uniform sampling never captured.

The reranker permutes \psm{}'s top-5, so Hit@5 is invariant by construction.
On the headline Nymeria session it adds localization precision, not recall: it lifts exemplar mIoU@5 from $0.074$ to $0.101$ ($+37\%$ relative) at \caponek{}$=K$, and \caponek{}$=1$+rerank reaches mIoU $0.069$, statistically indistinguishable from brute-force's $0.074$ (paired bootstrap $\Delta{=}{-}0.005$, 95\% CI $[-0.032,+0.021]$, $n{=}187$).
On Hit@1 the effect is bimodal: it tripled Hit@1 on \texttt{seq008\_running\_001} ($10\%{\to}30\%$) and helped on \texttt{seq009} ($+3.6$\,pp), but hurt on \texttt{seq003} ($-3.3$\,pp) and Nymeria ($-1.6$\,pp).
The rerank is a visually-rich-query lever, useful when visual content discriminates beyond \psm{}'s embedding similarity, counterproductive on action-narrations.
The headline result---\psm{}'s query-guided retrieval reaching sessions that uniform sampling misses---is achievable without any MLLM in the loop; we do not promote the reranker to a default.
